About the Book

What happens when the people you trust most are the very ones who cause you harm?

In Sins of the Father: How I Overcame the Effects of Incest—More or Less, Dr. David Maresh shares a deeply personal account of growing up in a home marked by physical, sexual, and emotional abuse. He writes openly about what he experienced as a child and how those experiences continued to affect him long after childhood had ended.

Through a collection of memories and personal reflections, Dr. Maresh takes readers through different periods of his life, revealing how the abuse shaped his relationships and the way he viewed himself. His story is not always easy to read, nor does he try to make it comfortable. Yet even in some of his most painful recollections, he finds moments of humor that bring a distinctly human quality to his writing.

As both a survivor and a psychiatrist, Dr. Maresh approaches his experiences with a perspective shaped by years of personal reflection and professional understanding. He discusses the role that therapy and faith have played in his life while acknowledging that recovery is rarely straightforward. Some wounds take years to understand, and others may never fully disappear.

Sins of the Father is ultimately about a man learning to live beyond what happened to him. It does not promise an easy path to healing or suggest that the past can simply be forgotten. Instead, it offers an honest account of what it means to keep moving forward, even when the effects of childhood trauma remain.

For those who have lived through similar experiences, or who wish to better understand someone who has, Dr. Maresh’s story offers a reminder that there is still hope for a meaningful life after abuse.

This memoir contains accounts of childhood sexual abuse, physical and emotional abuse, family violence, and suicidal thoughts. Read the full content note.
