Before You Read

Some stories are difficult to tell, and others are just as difficult to read.

Sins of the Father is a deeply personal account that asks readers to approach its pages with an open mind and an understanding of the experiences being shared.

In this memoir, Dr. David Maresh revisits moments from his childhood that continued to affect him well into adulthood. He writes candidly about what happened within his family, including memories that are painful and, at times, disturbing. His account is told through a series of personal recollections, allowing readers to see how these experiences shaped his understanding of himself and the people around him.

Dr. Maresh does not attempt to make his past easier to read. He shares what he remembers and acknowledges the difficulties of making sense of those memories. His experiences with psychotherapy and his later work as a psychiatrist also inform the way he reflects on his life.

For readers who have experienced abuse or have supported someone who has, certain passages may feel particularly close to home. There is no need to rush through them. This is one person’s story, and every reader may respond to it differently.

A Note on Sensitive Content

This memoir contains detailed accounts of childhood sexual abuse, physical and emotional abuse, family violence, and suicidal thoughts. Some passages may be distressing, particularly for readers with personal experiences of trauma.

Readers are encouraged to take their time and pause whenever needed.

If you need support right now: in the U.S., call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) or contact RAINN at 1-800-656-4673.

Dr. Robert David Maresh is a board-certified psychiatrist with more than 30 years of clinical experience. A fifth-generation Houstonian, he has spent much of his life working in the field of mental health, helping people understand and work through some of their most difficult experiences. He now resides in Ponte Vedra Beach, Florida.

Behind his years of medical practice is a personal history that has greatly influenced the way he sees the world. As a survivor of childhood sexual abuse, Dr. Maresh knows firsthand how deeply early experiences can affect a person’s life. His path has not always been easy, and he speaks openly about the struggles he has faced along the way.

“I am an ordinary guy, but my story is worth retelling and remembering.”

Having spent decades listening to the experiences of others, Dr. Maresh eventually decided to share his own. His writing reflects the same honesty he brings to his personal story, often finding room for humor even when looking back on painful memories. He does not claim to have all the answers or to have left every hardship behind. Instead, he believes there is value in speaking openly about experiences that many people find difficult to discuss.

Today, Dr. Maresh hopes that sharing his story will help others feel less alone and recognize that a painful past does not have to determine the course of their future.

About the Book

What happens when the people you trust most are the very ones who cause you harm?

In Sins of the Father: How I Overcame the Effects of Incest—More or Less, Dr. David Maresh shares a deeply personal account of growing up in a home marked by physical, sexual, and emotional abuse. He writes openly about what he experienced as a child and how those experiences continued to affect him long after childhood had ended.

Through a collection of memories and personal reflections, Dr. Maresh takes readers through different periods of his life, revealing how the abuse shaped his relationships and the way he viewed himself. His story is not always easy to read, nor does he try to make it comfortable. Yet even in some of his most painful recollections, he finds moments of humor that bring a distinctly human quality to his writing.

As both a survivor and a psychiatrist, Dr. Maresh approaches his experiences with a perspective shaped by years of personal reflection and professional understanding. He discusses the role that therapy and faith have played in his life while acknowledging that recovery is rarely straightforward. Some wounds take years to understand, and others may never fully disappear.

Sins of the Father is ultimately about a man learning to live beyond what happened to him. It does not promise an easy path to healing or suggest that the past can simply be forgotten. Instead, it offers an honest account of what it means to keep moving forward, even when the effects of childhood trauma remain.

For those who have lived through similar experiences, or who wish to better understand someone who has, Dr. Maresh’s story offers a reminder that there is still hope for a meaningful life after abuse.
